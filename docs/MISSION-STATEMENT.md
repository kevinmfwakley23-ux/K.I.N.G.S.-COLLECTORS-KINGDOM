# K.I.N.G.S. Collector's Kingdom — Engineering Mission Statement

## Permanent mission

Build K.I.N.G.S. Collector's Kingdom into the most capable, trustworthy, intelligent, useful, and enjoyable collector platform available while preserving the locked Construction Documents, collector ownership, security, accessibility, and real-world operability.

The Kingdom must not merely imitate existing collector products. It must understand what the best current products do well, identify where they fail collectors, adopt only ideas that strengthen the approved Kingdom architecture, and improve those ideas before they enter production.

The target is a collector platform with broader collectible coverage, stronger organization, better knowledge and memory, stronger AI assistance through K.I.N.G.S. AI, more trustworthy valuation evidence, better physical-inventory control, better marketplace workflows, better portability, and a more coherent experience than competing apps.

## Authority hierarchy

Every build session shall use this decision order:

1. **Locked K.I.N.G.S. Collector's Kingdom Construction Documents** — permanent product, architecture, PRD, infrastructure, engineering-standard, and implementation-sequence authority.
2. **Current repository architecture, tests, verified implementation, and recorded engineering decisions** — implementation truth for what already exists.
3. **Fresh competitor, GitHub, app-store, standards, API, and web research** — evidence for improvements, parity gaps, and better implementation ideas.

External research may improve how an approved Kingdom capability is implemented. It may not silently replace, contradict, reorder, or weaken a locked Construction Document requirement. If research reveals a genuinely superior idea that would require an architectural or product change, that change must be explicitly reconciled with the Construction Documents before implementation.

## Mandatory research-before-build protocol

Before every meaningful build session or milestone implementation, the engineer or AI development agent shall perform a fresh competitive and technical reconnaissance appropriate to the feature being built.

At minimum, research shall inspect:

1. Current official webpages and documentation for leading products related to the feature.
2. Current Google Play listings and, when relevant, App Store listings, including recent product changes and recurring user complaints.
3. Relevant active GitHub repositories and open-source implementations for architecture, workflow, testing, portability, privacy, scanning, data-management, or UX ideas.
4. Current public standards, APIs, data providers, security guidance, accessibility practices, and platform capabilities when they materially affect the feature.
5. Existing Collector's Kingdom Construction Documents, repository architecture, tests, and previous implementation decisions before any competing idea is adopted.

Research must be current enough to detect meaningful product changes. Old comparison articles alone are not sufficient when current first-party or store information is available.

## Competitive parity and superiority rule

For each feature area, determine what strong competitors currently provide and maintain a mental or written parity matrix covering at least:

- capability
- collector workflow
- data quality and freshness
- search and discovery
- scanning or intake
- organization and physical location
- valuation and market evidence
- collection history and portfolio analytics
- import/export and portability
- marketplace, trade, and seller workflows
- privacy and security
- cross-device behavior
- accessibility
- performance
- automation and AI assistance
- known user complaints or failure modes

Collector's Kingdom should provide equivalent capability where it serves the approved product mission, then improve it through stronger trust, better integration, less friction, broader usefulness, better evidence, or more intelligent assistance.

Parity does not require copying every competitor feature. Features that conflict with the Construction Documents, collector trust, privacy, security, maintainability, or the Kingdom identity shall be rejected or redesigned.

## Adoption standard

Competitive research is inspiration and evidence, not permission to copy proprietary implementation or visual design.

When adopting an idea:

1. Identify the collector problem the idea solves.
2. Confirm that the problem belongs within the approved Kingdom roadmap.
3. Design a Kingdom-native solution rather than cloning another product.
4. Improve at least one meaningful dimension when practical: reliability, clarity, speed, privacy, evidence quality, accessibility, interoperability, cost control, AI assistance, or collector control.
5. Respect software licenses and intellectual-property boundaries. Do not copy incompatible source code.
6. Add real tests and verification evidence.
7. Document important architectural or product consequences.

## No-fake-functionality rule

A competitor capability must never be represented as implemented merely to make the Kingdom appear competitive.

No fake counts, synthetic collection values presented as real, phantom integrations, decorative controls presented as functional, fabricated AI results, placeholder success paths, or unsupported marketplace data may be used to satisfy parity claims.

If a service is not yet built, the Kingdom must state that status honestly while preserving a clean path for the approved future implementation.

## Collector intelligence mission

K.I.N.G.S. AI is the shared intelligence core, but Collector's Kingdom remains the authority for collector identity, authorization, collection ownership, privacy, Vault records, marketplace rules, and product actions.

The intelligence experience should eventually exceed ordinary collector apps by combining:

- collector-controlled long-term memory
- room and workflow context
- collection knowledge
- provenance and ownership history
- market and valuation evidence
- visual identification support
- uncertainty handling and verification
- multiple-model contribution where useful
- user-selected cost/quality strategy
- weak-response filtering, verification, and adjudication before unreliable information becomes trusted output
- clear distinction between facts, estimates, recommendations, and uncertainty

The Keeper must use that intelligence as the collector's persistent royal assistant, butler, servant, advisor, steward, curator, and guide rather than behaving like a detached chatbot.

## Experience identity

The Kingdom must feel like a coherent place rather than a collection of generic dashboards.

- Castle interiors: elegant polished white marble with black and gold veining, refined modern royal-estate architecture, uncluttered layouts, and strong spatial orientation.
- The Keeper: an upright anthropomorphic lion who walks on two legs and wears refined royal service attire; visibly present and contextually useful throughout the Kingdom.
- The Vault: a grand, orderly, high-security treasure-vault environment designed around protection, organization, provenance, physical storage location, and presentation of the collector's treasures.
- The Marketplace: outside the castle as the Kingdom Street Market — an open-air royal collector market with organized stalls, awnings, display cases, merchant areas, and a refined farmers-market/street-market atmosphere.

Visual spectacle must never reduce usability, accessibility, performance, or trust.

## Current competitive lessons to preserve

The competitive research performed before IMP-004 reinforced several long-term priorities:

- Broad collectible coverage and custom collection types are essential for a true general collector platform.
- Fast camera, barcode, and bulk intake dramatically reduce collection-entry friction.
- Exact variant/condition handling is more important than confidently returning a wrong match; uncertain recognition should surface candidates or report that the item is not confidently identified.
- Physical-location tracking should support real storage structures such as rooms, cabinets, safes, display cases, shelves, binders, pages, pockets, boxes, rows, and dividers.
- Large collections require excellent search, filters, saved views, bulk operations, and direct location retrieval.
- Value history, purchase price, realized sale price, gains/losses, grading context, and source freshness should be evidence-based rather than opaque.
- Collectors should retain practical ownership of their data through import, export, backup, restore, and portable formats.
- Insurance/documentation reports, provenance records, audit history, reminders, and custody/location history can make a collection system more useful than a simple catalog.
- Marketplace workflows should allow a verified Vault item to become a listing without repeated data entry while preserving collector approval and transaction safeguards.
- Cross-device operation should not force the collector to rebuild or separately maintain the same collection.
- Privacy-preserving or local processing should be preferred when it materially improves collector control without destroying capability.

## Definition of engineering success

A build session succeeds only when it leaves the Kingdom measurably stronger and its claims can be demonstrated.

A milestone is complete only when:

- approved requirements are implemented
- relevant competitor parity gaps have been considered
- any adopted ideas are Kingdom-native and improved where practical
- functionality is real and integrated
- security and authorization boundaries remain intact
- accessibility and responsive behavior are preserved
- tests exercise meaningful behavior
- build, lint, type/contract, test, and production verification gates pass
- documentation matches implementation
- known limitations are stated honestly

The permanent question is not simply, "Did we add the feature?"

It is:

**Does this make Collector's Kingdom a stronger, more trustworthy, more capable home for collectors than the alternatives available today?**
